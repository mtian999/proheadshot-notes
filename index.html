What four photos buy you

The input ceiling on photo-based AI headshots, and why it decides what the tool can promise

Photo-based headshot tools tend to talk about the model and skip the input side. The binding constraint is not the generator, it is the ceiling on what you can hand over: four photographs of one person. Every promise the product can honestly make follows from that number.

Why four frames is the right ceiling

Four is enough to read a face from the angles that actually change a portrait — straight on, three-quarter left, three-quarter right, plus one looser frame that shows hairline and jaw in natural light. It is not enough to reconstruct a profile nobody supplied. When a result looks wrong, the usual cause is a missing angle rather than a weak model.

So the honest description is narrow: the tool restyles lighting, clothing and setting around a face you already own. It does not invent that face. A photo tool claiming a guaranteed likeness is selling something it cannot measure.

Three choices, then a price

The variables worth exposing are few, and all of them are decisions a person can make without seeing a render first:

  • Outfit — business, business casual, or a plain shirt
  • Scene — studio backdrop, office, or outdoor shade
  • Pose — straight on, slight turn, or arms crossed

Pick those three and the credit cost is knowable before anything renders. That ordering matters more than it sounds. On a paid tool with no trial tier, discovering the price after the render is the difference between a purchase and a trap, and a failed generation should never consume a paid resource.

What delivery looks like

Output arrives as a single image or a ZIP of the full set, at the resolution the chosen pack provides. Pricing runs on prepaid credits that expire: packs start at 7 USD for 4,000 credits, with 15 USD and 25 USD tiers and a 90 to 360 day validity window. There is also a studio subscription for people who need sets on a schedule.

That is the whole product. The narrowness is deliberate — a tool that only moves outfit, scene and pose cannot misrepresent the face it was given, and the four-photo ceiling is what keeps that promise checkable.
